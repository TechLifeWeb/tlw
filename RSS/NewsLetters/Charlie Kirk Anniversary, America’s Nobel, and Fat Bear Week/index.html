Kirk Assassination Anniversary

Today marks one year since conservative activist Charlie Kirk was fatally shot while speaking at Utah Valley University (read last year's write-up). His organization, Turning Point USA, returns to campus today for a memorial.

Kirk founded Turning Point in 2012 at age 18 and grew it into a nonprofit with an operating budget of over $100M. He is credited with helping to boost President Donald Trump’s support among voters under 30 from 36% in 2020 to 47% in 2024. Since Kirk's assassination, college students' support for violence as a means to combat speech has dropped slightly. His death has also fueled infighting among Republicans and sparked conspiracy theories across the political spectrum, with a 2025 poll finding 36% of voters distrust media coverage of the assassination. Kirk’s widow, Erika, now leads Turning Point, where new high school and college chapters have grown 168% and 64%, respectively. Read her op-ed on life without him here (free w/email).

Separately, a Utah judge last week ordered alleged gunman Tyler Robinson, 23, to stand trial on seven counts, including aggravated murder, which could make him eligible for the death penalty.

Lasker Award Winners

The Lasker Awards recognized breakthroughs yesterday spanning the biology of sleep, a blood disorder treatment, and Parkinson’s disease advocacy. The prestigious honors, known as "America's Nobel," include a $250K reward per category.

Emmanuel Mignot (Stanford) and Masashi Yanagisawa (University of Tsukuba) won the basic medical research award for separately finding that the absence of a brain chemical called orexin causes many cases of narcolepsy. Their discovery translated into an FDA-approved drug for the neurological disorder, which affects an estimated one in 2,000 people in the US. Kunihiro Hattori, Takehisa Kitazawa, and Tomoyuki Igawa shared the clinical medical research award for developing a drug to treat hemophilia A (explore their invention, via YouTube). Actor Michael J. Fox received the public service award for raising awareness and funds toward Parkinson’s research.

Established in 1945, the Lasker Foundation has honored over 400 recipients, more than 80 of whom have gone on to earn a Nobel Prize (scroll for map of winners’ home institutions).
