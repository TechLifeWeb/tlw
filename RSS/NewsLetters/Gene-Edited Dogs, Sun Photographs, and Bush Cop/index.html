Gene-Edited Beagles

A startup created two allergen-free beagles using CRISPR gene-editing technology, according to a study published yesterday. The advance could expand the number of pet owners, while potentially shifting demand from rescue operations toward breeders. Meet the scientists and pups here (w/video).

Scientists at Kindred Companion Sciences edited dog skin cells to disable the main gene responsible for producing allergy-causing proteins, which all dogs carry. They then inserted the edited genetic material into eggs, which were implanted into a beagle surrogate (see CRISPR 101). The surrogate birthed two puppies, Alfie and Bailey, whose saliva, hair, and dander do not contain the proteins. Alfie's and Bailey’s owners showed no allergic reaction to samples from the gene-edited beagles despite reacting to breeds that shed less. See 30 low-shed breeds.

Bailey and Alfie are being monitored for long-term health, though scientists say issues are unlikely. Dogs have naturally been born without the allergy-causing protein, albeit extremely rarely.

Turkey-Kurdish Peace Proposal

Turkey’s ruling coalition submitted a draft law yesterday outlining plans to secure peace with the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK). The proposal has broad support in parliament and is expected to advance to a debate this week.

The law would shield about 4,000 fighters from prosecution, namely those not charged with homicide who return to Turkey within six months. It comes after the PKK’s imprisoned leader called for an end to violence last year, as Syria’s new government has made headway in reintegrating its Kurdish region, where many PKK fighters have been based. Following their leader's announcement last year, 30 fighters burned their weapons in a symbolic gesture (watch video).

Since the 1980s, the PKK has launched an insurgency in pursuit of an independent Kurdish state or expanded rights and autonomy. The group is designated as a terrorist organization by Turkey, the US, and EU; violence has killed an estimated 40,000 people, many of them Kurdish. See the conflict in charts.

Have You Ever Stared Directly at the Sun?

Scientists have captured the highest resolution images of the sun to date, publishing them in a Nature study yesterday. The images also reveal the existence of a physical phenomenon that can help explain the dynamics behind space weather. See here.

The photographs were captured by the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope in Maui, Hawaii. Researchers had set out to test the telescope’s capabilities when photographs of the sun’s surface showed swirling, wave-like patterns. Those shapes confirmed the presence of Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities, a phenomenon that guides how fluids move, and that, in this case, indicated magnetized plasma passing each other at different speeds (how it works, w/video). While the instabilities had long been theorized to take place on the sun, they have never before been observed at a granular level of detail.

Solar activity can impact daily functions on Earth, including damaging satellites and causing radio blackouts. Learn more here.
