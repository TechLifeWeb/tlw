Migrant Protections End

More than 300,000 Haitians and roughly 4,000 Syrians have lost their ability to work and live in the US legally after a temporary protected status expired yesterday. The lapse comes after a Supreme Court ruling in June empowered the Trump administration to end protections.

Haiti—the Western Hemisphere's poorest nation—first received temporary protected status in 2010 following a deadly 7.0 magnitude earthquake. Haiti has since faced overlapping crises, including widespread gang violence (with armed groups controlling about 85% of the capital, Port-au-Prince) and repeated political upheaval, including the 2021 assassination of President Jovenel Moïse. Syria first received TPS in 2012 after civil war erupted, and despite the fall of the decades-long dictatorship of Bashar al-Assad in 2024, parts of the country remain unstable.

Nearly 200,000 Haitians and 2,000 Syrians with TPS are part of the workforce, with the two groups contributing roughly $5.9B to the US economy. See estimated breakdown here (use drop-down to filter by country).

New World Heritage Sites

UNESCO has added D-Day’s landing beaches and Mount Olympus to its list of World Heritage sites this week, among others—including three added on an emergency basis. The status signals the sites have universal value—natural, cultural, or both—and are worthy of preservation.

The designation program dates back to the 1970s, when Ecuador’s Galápagos Islands and the US’ Yellowstone National Park were among the first 12 sites to receive the honor (see photos from US sites). Since then, member countries have nominated hundreds of sites in an effort to attract tourism, raise conservation funds, or both. A decade after its 2008 listing, Mount Sanqingshan National Park in China saw annual visitors jump from 1.5 million to 24 million. Explore a map of the more than 1,200 sites here.

The World Heritage Committee is composed of representatives from more than 20 countries on a rotating basis. To be accepted, a nominated site must meet at least one of 10 criteria.

Chilean Fugitive Arrested

Chilean police have captured a fugitive convicted of kidnapping and murdering musician and activist Victor Jara 53 years ago.

Nelson Haase Mazzei, 80, fled in 2023 after he and six other former soldiers were convicted in absentia of murdering Jara and a Chilean official in 1973. Jara was a leading figure in Chile's New Song movement, which backed socialist politician Salvador Allende's successful 1970 presidential bid. The murder occurred five days after Augusto Pinochet overthrew Allende in a military coup and ushered in a nearly 17-year dictatorship (w/photos). Jara's widow and daughter pushed to reopen the murder case nearly a decade after Pinochet's dictatorship ended, ultimately leading to the 2023 convictions. Haase Mazzei was the last fugitive; one of the other men died by suicide, and the remaining five are serving prison sentences.

Jara's 1971 song "The Right to Live in Peace" (listen here), originally an anti-Vietnam War anthem, was revived during Chile's 2019 protests against economic inequality.
