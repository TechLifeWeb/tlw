Apple's Upgrade Push

Apple yesterday announced a new option for customers to lease iPhones, iPads, Macs, and Apple Watches. The move came as the company briefly hit a $5T valuation after surpassing Nvidia in market value earlier this week.

The program, called Upgrade, involves a partnership with buy-now-pay-later company Klarna. Customers can lease iPhones for up to two years, starting at $17.99 per month (the iPhone 17 Pro will cost up to $45.99 per month). At the one- or two-year mark, customers can purchase their existing device for an additional fee, upgrade it, or return it. Estimates show the average iPhone replacement cycle has grown to nearly four years; if the leasing option is successful, a portion of Apple’s customers are expected to upgrade almost twice as often. The program may also allow Apple to better spread out revenue across quarters.

$20B FIFA Spinoff

FIFA, soccer's nonprofit global governing body, is planning to create a $20B for-profit spinoff to run commercial and event operations, including the World Cup.

Under the plan, FIFA would retain exclusive control over football governance, the match calendar and competitions, as well as regulatory and sporting decisions. Noncontrolling stakes of up to 20% would be offered to private investors to raise up to $4.2B. One of the lead investors will reportedly be a venture capital fund founded by Joshua Kushner, the brother of President Donald Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner. FIFA says the capital raise would be used to expand development funding for its 211 national member associations. Europe's soccer federation criticized the plan, accusing FIFA of commercializing soccer.

A Finger Lickin’ Auction

Personal items and property belonging to KFC founder Colonel Harland Sanders went up for auction yesterday in Kentucky, marking the first time the personal collection was offered publicly.

The sale featured 68 pieces of memorabilia from Sanders and his wife, Claudia, including his signature black clip-on bow tie, engraved pressure cookers, personal watches, an original cash register, and a bust sculpted by his daughter. Among the most notable items is Sanders' 1964 day planner, containing handwritten notes and a list of 11 herbs and spices that later became the subject of a 2001 lawsuit by KFC seeking to keep the contents private.

The couple's three-acre Shelbyville property—home to the 5,000-square-foot Blackwood Hall mansion and the still-operating Claudia Sanders Dinner House—is being sold via sealed-bid auction, with final bids due Friday. See the property here.
