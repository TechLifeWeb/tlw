10 unbelievable unis that were actually worn during games

Thursday, November 05

UNIFORMLY EXCEPTIONAL THREADS #

They took the field in those jerseys?!

No corner of the sports world has prompted this incredulous query more than Minor League Baseball, whose teams regularly wear outlandish theme jerseys in addition to their regular uniforms. To be a Minor League fan is to be exposed, on a daily basis, to a cornucopia of bold colors and bolder design.

Friday is National Jersey Day, of course, so what better time than now to revisit some of Minor League Baseball's strangest-ever offerings? Ten examples can be found below, all of which debuted during the era of sartorial excess that was the 2010s. Let's take a trip down memory lane, marveling anew at these groundbreaking and bizarre Minor League jerseys.

(Note: Teams are listed at their current affiliation and level of play, which may have changed since the time these jerseys were first worn.)

Bacon-themed uniforms (Leigh Valley IronPigs, Triple-A PHI, 2014) 
Like Dylan going electric in 1965, the IronPigs' bacon-themed duds were an epochal event that firmly delineated the start of a new era. These uniforms, featuring bacon-strip hats and jerseys with a strip of bacon underneath a "'Pigs" wordmark, planted the seed for the food-themed alternate identity craze that continues in the Minor Leagues to this day. Feel the sizzle.

Organ Donor Night (Memphis Redbirds, Triple-A STL, 2011)
"It's what's inside that counts." The Redbirds took that message to heart, literally, when they wore innards-exposing jerseys as part of their Organ Donor Night promotion. Fans could sign up to be organ donors at the ballpark and, because this is Minor League Baseball, it didn't stop there. There was organ music throughout, and a local music store donated an organ that was won by a lucky fan.

Bark in the Park (El Paso Chihuahuas, Triple-A SD, 2014)
When the El Paso Chihuahuas played their inaugural season in 2014, their mission was to "live the brand." This mentality led to a sensational Bark in the Park jersey, featuring the piercing, plaintive stare of a larger-than-life chihuahua. "I'll never forget the first time I saw it,” said Chihuahuas general manager Brad Seymour. "It's perfect." The team won MiLB.com's Promo of the Year for this effort, which gained new attention in 2025 after it was featured as part of a viral "Last Week Tonight with John Oliver" segment about Minor League Baseball.

Social Media Night (Fort Wayne TinCaps, High-A SD, 2013)
In 1960, the Chicago White Sox became the first team to feature names on the backs of their uniforms. In 2013, the Fort Wayne TinCaps became the first team to feature 6,000 names. On Social Media Night, the team took the field wearing @TinCaps jerseys featuring the handles of nearly everyone who followed them on Twitter. It was a simpler time.

Portlandia Night (Eugene Emeralds, High-A SF, 2015)
Portlandia, a hipster-skewering sketch show on the IFC Network, was part of the zeitgeist in 2015. The Emeralds, who play in an Oregon city they consider to be just as weird as Portland, celebrated with a flannel and skinny jeans uniform ensemble. These outlandish duds, which did not include actual flannel or actual denim, were a sketch comedy premise come to life.

Zombie Apocalypse Night (Inland Empire 66ers, Single-A Mariners, 2014)
The most over-the-top theme jersey of all time? On Zombie Apocalypse Night, the 66ers took the field looking like they themselves were zombies who had just indulged in a horrifying feast. It was for a good cause, of course: The 66ers hosted a blood drive at the ballpark and proceeds from the jersey auction benefited a local blood donation center. Those who wanted to avoid the undead hordes were invited to sit in a designated "Quarantine Zone" seating area.

Back to the Future Doubleheader (Toledo Mud Hens, Triple-A DET, 2015)
The Mud Hens made ample use of the DeLorean for their innovative “Back to the Future” doubleheader. It kicked off with a "Back" game, featuring throwback '80s jerseys as well as vintage videoboard graphics. This was followed by a "Future" game, in which the jerseys were an interpretation of what the team might be wearing in 2035. Will this vision come to pass? We’ll find out soon enough.

Asparagus Night (Stockton Ports, Single-A A’s, 2016)
Stockton, in case you didn’t know, prides itself on being “The Asparagus Capital of the World.” The Ports have long celebrated this versatile vegetable at the ballpark, including in 2016 when they suited up in green-on-green jerseys featuring, per MiLB.com’s Josh Jackson, “humongous stalks of asparagus shooting up the torso.”

Better Call Saul Night (Albuquerque Isotopes, Triple-A COL, 2016)
"Breaking Bad" and its spinoff "Better Call Saul" were both shot in Albuquerque. The latter series was in production in 2016, when the Isotopes took the field wearing jerseys featuring a giant photo of Bob Odenkirk as Jimmy McGill (the man who would one day become Saul Goodman). Odenkirk himself was in attendance, looking like a walking billboard for his character's unscrupulous legal services.

David Bowie Tribute (Bowie Baysox, Double-A BAL, 2017)
The Bowie Baysox, now known as the Chesapeake Baysox, didn't need to change their name for this 2017 theme night. They just needed to change the pronunciation. The team honored David Bowie, and what would have been his 70th birthday, by playing as the Boh-ee (as opposed to Boo-ie) Baysox. The evening's uniforms were described as "an amalgamation of a Ziggy Stardust outfit and the Union Jack flag." But where were the spiders?

ALTERNATE IDENTITY UNVEILING SEASON HAS BEGUN #

Forty-six alternate identities debuted in 2026. What will 2027 bring? Well, we're beginning to find out. Please welcome the following entities to the Minor League Baseball landscape.

Amarillo Chiltons (Amarillo Sod Poodles)
The Chilton is a cocktail that originated in the Texas Panhandle, consisting of vodka, lemon juice and soda water in a salt-rimmed glass. Legend has it that it was first ordered at a country club bar by one Dr. Chilton. This mysterious doctor probably didn't receive his drink in an anthropomorphic glass topped with a lemon wedge in the shape of Texas, but there's nothing wrong with a little artistic license.

Silicon Valley Space Unicorns (San Jose Giants)
Many professional sports teams play within Silicon Valley's broad parameters, but it took until now for one to incorporate Silicon Valley into its name. A Space Unicorn is a startup business now valued at over $1 billion; thus, this interstellar alternate identity is an embodiment of "San Jose's ambitious, inventive spirit, celebrating a community of dreamers and pioneers who continue to transform science fiction into reality."

Yes, San Jose is the place where dreams come true!

The above photo was taken at Excite Ballpark, home of the San Jose Giants. That stadium is a blast from the past as opposed to a vision from the future, and all the better for it. When I visited in 2016 it was my great honor to take part in the (sadly discontinued) "Smash for Cash!" between-inning contest. Throwing a baseball at the headlights of a dilapidated bread delivery truck is a refreshingly analog endeavor.
