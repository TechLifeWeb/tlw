Hunt for Earhart’s Plane

An expedition to find the plane Amelia Earhart disappeared in is set to begin this October. The news was announced yesterday, on what would have been Earhart’s 129th birthday.

A 16-person team of archaeologists, filmmakers, and other experts will travel by boat on Oct. 7 to a remote island in the central Pacific nation of Kiribati. The expedition believes Earhart and her navigator, Fred Noonan, got stranded there while trying to circumnavigate the globe in 1937. Radio signals, human bones, and 1930s-era artifacts—including anti-freckle cream—all suggest Earhart and Noonan may have died on an unplanned stop at the uninhabited island. Shortly after a strong tropical storm in 2015, satellite imagery also captured a visual anomaly the expedition team believes could be the wreckage of Earhart's long-lost Lockheed 10-E Electra aircraft. Other experts say it's likely a downed coconut tree. See the satellite image here.

Before her fatal 1937 mission, Earhart became the first woman to fly solo and nonstop across the Atlantic Ocean, a college professor, and a fashion designer.

LeBron James to play record-extending 24th NBA season with the 76ers.

The legendary 41-year-old forward said yesterday that he signed a two-year, $8M deal. James joins the Philadelphia-based team vying to win his fifth league championship and break the 76ers' 43-year championship drought. He broke a 52-year drought for the city of Cleveland in 2016, after beginning his NBA career with the team in 2003, directly out of high school. (More, w/X announcement posts)

Trump attends rescheduled White House Correspondents' Association Dinner.

President Donald Trump and members of the media gathered in Washington, DC's Waldorf Astoria hotel for the annual gala, which was rescheduled after a shooter opened fire at the original event in April. The association honored the Secret Service officer who was shot in a protective vest, as well as the Washington Hilton staff who worked that night. Mentalist Oz Pearlman also returned as yesterday evening’s entertainer. (More)

Measles cases reach a 35-year high, surpassing last year's total.

Federal officials yesterday reported 2,318 confirmed measles cases across 43 states and Washington, DC, so far this year. Roughly 93% of the cases are among unvaccinated people, and nearly 69% are among those under age 20. No deaths have been reported. Last year saw 2,289 confirmed cases, with three deaths. A panel of international experts is set to meet in November to discuss whether the US can keep its designation as a country that officially eliminated the disease. Ugandan park rangers relocate endangered giraffes to escape oil drilling.

Rangers this week began relocating 12 critically endangered Nubian giraffes from Uganda's largest national park, which also holds roughly 40% of the country's oil deposits. A French company began drilling in 2023, and activity has increased in recent months. The giraffes were transported about 65 miles by truck to the Ajai Wildlife Reserve, which is trying to rebuild its wildlife population after poaching and political instability nearly wiped out the population. (More, w/video)

Linguists say they've discovered the "golden age" of languages.

Humans spoke tens of thousands of languages between 1,000 and 3,000 years ago, according to a study published this week. This so-called golden age ended with the rise of large states and empires, such as the Roman Empire, which compelled people to adopt their languages. The study challenges the long-held theory that language extinction began 500 years ago with European colonialism. (More, w/linguistic map)
