Mississippi Race Set

The Associated Press called Mississippi's Senate race for Scott Colom (D) yesterday after counties updated their results amid ballot review and processing. The latest tally puts Colom ahead of incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith (R) by over 4,600 votes, or roughly 0.5% of the total. Hyde-Smith has conceded.

Colom will become the state's first Black senator since the post-Civil War Reconstruction era. Mississippi was the first state to send a Black senator to Congress, with Sen. Hiram Revels (R-MS) serving from 1870 to 1871. Sen. Blanche Bruce (R-MS) served from 1875 to 1881. No Black senators have been elected from the state in the years since, though Mississippi boasts the largest proportion of Black residents of any state (37.9%). Colom's victory also marks the first Democratic senator elected from Mississippi in over 40 years.

Democrats were late to inject cash into the race, with Mississippi not widely considered a pickup opportunity. One viral ad campaign by Colom centered on Hyde-Smith's comments on the price of beef; watch it here (via YouTube).

Russia-Ukraine Talks Wrap

Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle to a large prisoner-of-war exchange yesterday after two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended without a major breakthrough toward ending the nearly five-year war.

The exchange could occur within two to three weeks, with the number of prisoners to be finalized today (how the countries exchange POWs). The talks marked the countries' first direct negotiations in nine months, with disagreements remaining over Russian-occupied Ukrainian territory and postwar security guarantees. European officials were excluded at Moscow’s insistence but consulted with Ukraine’s delegation on the sidelines. The Kremlin opposes Europe's military and financial support for Ukraine, while Ukraine wants its European allies involved. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff will meet Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow today before meeting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin.

The war has displaced nearly 10 million Ukrainians and become Europe's deadliest conflict since World War II. Track the conflict on an interactive map here.

The Coachella Economy

Pop star Dua Lipa, psychedelic pop act Tame Impala, and R&B artist SZA will headline the 2027 Coachella music festival, organizers revealed yesterday. See the full lineup here.

Since its 1999 debut, Coachella has evolved into a major pop culture tastemaker. (It wasn't an overnight success; read history.) The 2026 festival drew roughly 125,000 daily attendees, with weekend passes starting around $600. The rise of festival-going reflects the growing appeal of the experience economy, with two-thirds of 18- to 35-year-olds saying they'd rather attend a live event than buy a product of a similar value. Musicians are also increasingly relying on live music revenue as album sales fall and digital streaming payouts remain low. Headliners can command up to $5M per weekend, while smaller artists reportedly earn closer to $10K.
