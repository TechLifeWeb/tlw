California Voting Crackdown

Federal officials arrested more than two dozen people in California accused of voting illegally in US elections. The crackdown comes two weeks before the US midterm elections on Nov. 3.

All of the people arrested are lawful permanent residents but not US citizens, the Justice Department says. They are on a list of more than 20,000 names the DOJ tagged as “likely” noncitizens to whom California mailed ballots this year. The DOJ last week sued the state to prevent election officials from counting those ballots until immigration status is confirmed; California says the DOJ did not provide any evidence to support the allegations.

Since 1996, 73 noncitizens have been convicted of voting illegally. The Census Bureau this year suggested upward of 24,000 voters in the 2020 election were noncitizens; see criticisms of that finding.

Saudi Bugging Suspect

Federal prosecutors yesterday asked a judge to detain a Saudi national accused of bugging OpenAI's headquarters until his trial, citing his alleged ties to Saudi royals and plans to flee the country.

The FBI arrested Abdulrahman Alfozan earlier this month on suspicion of planting an audio recording device in an executive conference room to capture proprietary information. Prosecutors say an undisclosed member of the Saudi royal family repeatedly sought to recruit the 35-year-old engineer, who joined OpenAI in 2025, to an AI venture in the kingdom. Alfozan reportedly bought one-way tickets to Saudi Arabia for his family weeks before the devices were installed. His defense says he planned to spend his roughly two-month paternity leave there. After his arrest, Saudi officials purchased new tickets for his wife and son.

Separately, Saudi Arabia postponed its flagship investment conference until 2027 following airport attacks by Iran-backed Houthis. AI was expected to feature prominently as the kingdom's AI company, HUMAIN, seeks $2.5B for data center development.

Israeli Settler Attacks

Israeli police arrested 10 people yesterday in connection with settler violence in the occupied West Bank over the weekend. The incidents included attacks on a US citizen and European diplomats who were visiting Palestine during its annual olive harvest.

A group of settlers assaulted a Washington Post photojournalist Friday while she was working. She suffered a broken rib, four broken vertebrae, and a nearly severed finger. Settlers attacked a convoy with Belgian, French, and Polish representatives the next day, damaging a car and beating a French mayor with metal bars. Last weekend's assaults are among roughly 200 olive-harvest related attacks since the start of September, according to the United Nations. The organization also reports that five Palestinians have been killed and more than 240 wounded by Israeli forces or settlers in the occupied West Bank over the past two weeks.

Olives constitute 50%-90% of farmers’ earnings in the West Bank. See how settler violence has impacted the groves here.
