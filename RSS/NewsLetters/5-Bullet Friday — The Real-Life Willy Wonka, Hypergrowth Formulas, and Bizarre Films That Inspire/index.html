Quote I’m pondering #

“Happiness is the absence of the striving for happiness.”
​― Adapted from Chuang Tzu, one of the founding figures of Daoism.
​
This is the first half of 至樂無樂, 至譽無譽 (Zhì lè wú lè, Zhì yù wú yù), which is decently translated as “Highest happiness [is] no happiness; Highest praise/renown [is] no praise/renown.”
​
The context in Chapter 18 of Zhuangzi is what makes me comfortable with the above English translation, which I love. Chuang Tzu’s point, as far as I understand it, is that when you empty yourself of the conscious striving to be happy or famous, you achieve something far better. Daoist lè (happiness, joy), rather than being a spike in emotion caused by getting what you want, is a steady undercurrent of contentment. The spiritual cousin of this quote, often misattributed to Nathaniel Hawthorne, actually comes from an anonymous “L.” in a New Orleans newspaper from 1848: “Happiness is a butterfly, which when pursued, seems always just beyond your grasp; but if you sit down quietly, may alight upon you.”
