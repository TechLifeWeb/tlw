One Big Headline

Evolution's Viral Twist

A viral pandemic that spread roughly five to seven million years ago may be responsible for differences between human and chimpanzee brains, according to a study published this week.

Chimpanzees are humans' closest evolutionary relatives, sharing nearly 99% of the same genes. Yet, the human brain is roughly three times larger, a difference whose origins have puzzled scientists for decades (scroll for diagram). The new study identified a fragment of viral DNA present in chimpanzees but absent in humans that appears to impair genes vital to brain development. Researchers believe an ancient retrovirus, similar to HIV, inserted its genetic material into the primates' genomes, allowing it to be passed through subsequent generations. Humans, however, may have been protected by an antiviral protein that blocked the virus, ultimately giving them an evolutionary advantage.

Separately, another study out this month found that chimpanzees are less likely to steal food from their friends than their enemies. Experts say the finding may shed light on the roots of fairness and friendship.

Justice Department sues to block Alaska Senate results if incumbent loses.

Federal lawyers argue another candidate with the same name as incumbent Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan could mislead voters. Alaska's Supreme Court previously rejected an attempt to remove the other Dan Sullivan from the ballot. Recent polls show former Democratic Rep. Mary Peltola narrowly ahead of Sen. Sullivan. (More)

Separately, the Justice Department charged two former FBI officials with lying to Congress about their roles in the agency's Hunter Biden investigations. (More)

New York City bans horse-drawn carriages in Central Park.

The city council voted to eliminate all horse-drawn carriages by June 2028, ending a yearslong battle between animal welfare activists and a local transportation union (debate 101, w/video). The fight intensified this summer after an 18-year-old tourist died in a carriage accident. The ban leaves questions about the livelihoods of roughly 200 carriage industry workers and the care of 180 carriage horses. (More)

Melania Trump helps broker localized Russia-Ukraine ceasefire, White House says.

The temporary ceasefire will reportedly allow civilian evacuations and aid deliveries to Oleshky next week. The Ukrainian city lacks electricity, heat, food, and running water, with several roads impassable. As winter approaches, conditions are growing more precarious for the roughly 2,000 residents—down from 24,000 before the war. (More)

Separately, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Starlink isn't fully functional, despite reports President Donald Trump asked Elon Musk to lift restrictions. (More)

Israel orders full lockdown of occupied West Bank after shooting attack.

The Israeli military said two of its soldiers were killed in a shoot-out that also left two suspected Palestinian gunmen dead. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said soldiers will conduct home searches for "terrorist infrastructure" in the Hebron neighborhood where the suspects lived. The operation comes ahead of Israel's general election, which will determine whether Netanyahu stays in power. (More)

French officials deploy 70,000 law enforcement officers ahead of planned protests.

The Louvre and several other major museums will be closed as demonstrators plan to take the streets again to protest rising living costs (see France's economy in eight charts). At least 3,000 storefronts were looted or vandalized in last week's protests. The US State Department has issued a security warning for Americans traveling to Paris for tomorrow's NFL game between the Pittsburgh Steelers and New Orleans Saints. (More)

Final tests begin for the new James Bond.

Three to five actors have reportedly reached the final audition round to play James Bond in Amazon MGM Studios' upcoming movie directed by "Dune" filmmaker Denis Villeneuve. The men vying to replace Daniel Craig are believed to include "Slow Horses" actor Jack Lowden, "The Crown" actor Josh O'Connor, and "Fantastic Beasts" actor Callum Turner. (More)
