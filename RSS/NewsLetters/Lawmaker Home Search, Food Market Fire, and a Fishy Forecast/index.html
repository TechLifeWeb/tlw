Rep. Omar's Home Search

Homeland Security Investigations agents searched the Washington, DC, home of Rep. Ilhan Omar (D, MN-5) yesterday. The raid occurred 22 days before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, despite long-standing Justice Department guidance advising prosecutors to refrain from publicly investigating candidates within 60 days of an election.

Omar has represented Minnesota’s 5th District, which includes Minneapolis, since 2019 and is seeking a fifth term this cycle. She was born in Somalia in 1982, arrived in the US as a refugee at age 12, and became a naturalized citizen in 2000. The search was reportedly part of a yearslong investigation into alleged immigration fraud stemming from paperwork her late father completed when she was a child. The Trump administration has repeatedly questioned Omar’s immigration status and alleged she married her brother to help him obtain citizenship. She has denied all accusations.

Polls predict Omar has a 99% probability of winning reelection in November over the Republican nominee John Nagel.

Russell Brand on Trial

British comedian and YouTuber Russell Brand appeared in a London court yesterday for the start of a trial on rape, sexual assault, and indecent assault charges involving six women.

Brand, 51, faces seven charges for events that allegedly occurred between 1999 and 2009, including raping a woman during a British Labour Party conference. The charges came after a 2023 investigative documentary examined sexual assault allegations made against the comedian by multiple women from 2006 to 2013 (watch trailer, warning—sensitive). Brand, who denies any wrongdoing, has been barred from making revenue on YouTube since the documentary spurred an investigation into his conduct. However, his account gained 140,000 subscribers and his videos saw 31% more likes over the following four months.

After starting his career at the BBC and MTV, Brand more than doubled his YouTube following in 2021 by voicing COVID-19 skepticism. He now boasts roughly 6.6 million followers and continues to post conspiracy theories about topics ranging from 9/11 to Jeffrey Epstein.

Historic Food Market Fire

A fire destroyed Paris’ oldest covered food market early yesterday. It is not clear what started the fire at the popular tourist destination, which sits at the heart of the city's historic neighborhood, Le Marais.

The Enfants Rouges (“red children”) market was founded in 1615, during the reign of King Louis XIII. In the late 1700s, the market was named after a nearby orphanage whose children wore red uniforms. In the 1980s and ‘90s, residents blocked a plan to turn Enfants Rouges—then in disrepair—into a parking lot; the market reopened as a food court in 2000. This year, Food & Wine named it among the world’s top 10 global market destinations. All 17 stalls were destroyed in yesterday’s fire. See a photograph of the market from 1898, and a painting from 1907.

Separately, a cyberattack caused refrigerators at two French supermarket chains—Intermarché and Coopérative U—to defrost, the retailers said yesterday. More than 200 shops were affected.
