One Big Headline

Mangione Pleads Guilty

Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty yesterday to federal stalking charges in the 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson in midtown Manhattan. He is set to be sentenced on Dec. 18 and could face up to life in prison.

The 28-year-old University of Pennsylvania graduate told the court he posed as an investor to confirm the location of UnitedHealthcare's annual conference, outside of which he ultimately shot 50-year-old Thompson. Mangione said he was motivated by years of struggling to navigate the health insurance system while suffering from severe back pain; prosecutors claim he was never a UnitedHealthcare customer. Evidence found on the scene included shell casings with "delay," "deny," and "depose" written on them—an apparent reference to criticism of how insurers handle claims. See photos of more evidence here, including a handwritten to-do list.

Mangione’s defense team has filed a motion to have murder and weapons charges in a separate state trial dismissed under New York's double jeopardy law. The state trial is currently scheduled to begin Sept. 8.

Quick Hits

Trump takes White House ballroom construction to Supreme Court.

Trump officials yesterday petitioned the high court to allow the White House to resume its $400M ballroom project. A lower court halted the project last week, ruling it requires congressional approval. The Trump administration says the project is a national security matter, citing the need for a more secure event space following incidents like the White House Correspondents' Association dinner shooting. (More)

Separately, the Trump administration announced the arrest of a woman on felony charges for allegedly vandalizing the World War II Memorial in Washington, DC.

US missionary freed after nine months in captivity in Niger.

Kevin Rideout, 50, is in good health and back in US custody after being kidnapped by three armed individuals in Niger's capital city, Niamey, in October. As of this writing, no armed group has claimed responsibility and the details of Rideout's release are unknown. Niger has been ruled by a military junta since 2023 and has long faced attacks from militants linked to al-Qaida and the Islamic State organization. (More)

Jeff Bezos buys a minority stake in Liverpool FC.

A group of investors, including Bezos and Facebook cofounder Eduardo Saverin, bought around 30% of the Premier League club yesterday. The deal values the team at just over $7B and marks the Amazon founder's first foray into sports ownership. It comes days after venture capitalist Joshua Kushner and former Disney CEO Bob Iger bought the Los Angeles Lakers for a record $12.5B; why are billionaires buying sports teams? (More)

Spain removes remains of 11th-century kings as wildfires spread.

An emergency military unit temporarily relocated the remains of three kings and some historical paintings from a monastery threatened by a wildfire in northeastern Spain to a museum 50 miles south. The rescue operation comes as over 1.4 million acres have been burned by wildfires this year, fueled by record heat and persistent drought. (More)

Over in Sicily, more than 700 flights have been canceled this week as Mount Etna—Europe's most active volcano—spews an unusually large amount of ash. (w/photos)

Tiny satellite will listen for a faint whisper from the very early universe.

Researchers are developing a satellite the size of a carry-on suitcase to explore the period between the big bang and the appearance of the universe's first stars, according to a study published this week. The CosmoCube will orbit the far side of the moon to block interference from Earth as it searches for never-before-observed signals from hydrogen atoms in the early universe. (More)

Italian police recover $10M worth of paintings stolen in three-minute heist.

The paintings by Renoir, Cézanne, and Matisse were stolen from a private museum north of Parma, Italy, in March. Authorities yesterday said the works were recovered during searches ordered by prosecutors, but provided no further details. They also released security footage from the heist (watch via X). Meanwhile, police in Brazil this week recovered eight Matisse illustrations stolen in December. (More, w/photos)
