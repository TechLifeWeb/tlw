Presidential Plane Plot

President Donald Trump hid in a container to switch planes while leaving Turkey last month, US officials have revealed. See footage of Trump boarding an older Air Force One model before covertly exiting via a catering truck. (via YouTube)

Trump told reporters at the time they would return via the baby blue Air Force One as opposed to a newer model gifted by Qatar for “old time’s sake.” (See inside the newer jet compared with the older model.) From there, Trump was secretly transferred onto a military jet and taken to Britain. The subterfuge was designed to avoid a potential missile threat from Iran. Journalists and White House staff members were not told about the threat or that Trump had left the plane they were on. Trump was then secretly transferred back onto it when the two groups landed in the UK.

The last known time a plane decoy was used was in 2000, when then-President Bill Clinton flew from India to Pakistan.

A Celestial Doubleheader

A total solar eclipse will sweep across the Arctic and parts of Europe today, the first visible from mainland Europe in nearly 30 years. Totality will last less than two and a half minutes at its longest.

The path of totality crosses northern Russia, Greenland, Iceland, Spain, and a sliver of Portugal, while a partial eclipse will be visible across much of Europe, Canada, and parts of the northern US. A total eclipse occurs when the moon passes between Earth and the sun, temporarily blocking the sun from view; visualize the path of every eclipse from 1900 to 2099. Eclipses can also affect wildlife behavior, with researchers documenting bees going quiet, owls hooting, and crickets chirping as the sky darkens. Learn about the different types of eclipses here.

Separately, the Perseid meteor shower peaks tonight, potentially producing up to 100 meteors per hour under dark, new moon skies as Earth passes through debris left by Comet Swift-Tuttle. See the Perseids from space here.

Girls Without Schools

Approximately 2.4 million Afghan girls remain barred from secondary education five years into Taliban rule, according to a United Nations report published yesterday. Afghanistan is the only country in the world with formal bans on women's education.

The Taliban regained power in Afghanistan in August 2021, as US and NATO troops withdrew following two decades of war (Taliban 101). By March 2022, the Islamic fundamentalist group had shuttered girls' secondary schools, and women were barred from universities in December 2022. The decision erased recent gains in female education, with girls' secondary school enrollment rising from a few thousand in 2001 to roughly 1 million in 2021. The UN believes the bans could reduce the female workforce by 600,000 over the next 40 years and increase the risk of early marriage. Women are also prohibited from teaching boys, contributing to a teacher shortage. Afghanistan’s literacy rate is among the world’s lowest at roughly 37%. Compare literacy rates here.
