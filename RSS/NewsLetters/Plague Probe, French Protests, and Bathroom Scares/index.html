Russian Plague Investigation

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called on Russia to release more information about the death of a lab technician. The pressure comes as the World Health Organization yesterday said a possible case of pneumonic plague in Russia presents a “very low” risk to other countries.

Russian officials say there is no evidence the 28-year-old technician died of pneumonic plague, a rare and severe respiratory infection spread via airborne droplets. The disease responds to antibiotics, but is otherwise nearly 100% fatal. Russian officials have placed nearly 200 other individuals under medical supervision and imposed quarantine protocols at a local hospital (scroll for map). The research institute reportedly has plague specimens for outbreak prevention research. Pneumonic plague is the deadliest form of plague, the disease behind the Black Death, which killed roughly one-third of Europe's population between 1346 and 1353.

The technician's death has resurfaced concerns about lab safety, including debates over gain-of-function research giving viruses new properties, potentially making them more lethal or contagious. See arguments for and against here.

French School Protests

France saw its largest day of school protests yet, as demonstrators nationwide demanded more education funding amid deteriorating conditions in the country’s high schools.

The protests began at a school outside Paris last month and spread nationwide, with students blocking hundreds of schools yesterday. Demonstrators cite teacher shortages, overcrowded classrooms, and deteriorating facilities (what do they want?); secondary schools have 250,000 more students but fewer teachers than in 2008. France’s Interior Ministry estimated 250,000 people joined yesterday’s demonstrations, while organizers put the figure at 450,000. More than 6,000 people, mostly minors, have been arrested, with critics accusing police of a heavy-handed response after one student lost a hand and another lost use of an eye. Damage to Paris-area schools is estimated at $7.8M. See photos here.

Separately, Quebec’s separatist Parti Québécois won 59 of 127 seats in Monday’s provincial election, falling short of a majority but returning to power with a promise to hold a referendum on independence from Canada in 2029 or 2030.

Spy vs. Spy

Germany's 80-year-old former spy chief, August Hanning, was arrested yesterday on suspicion of espionage, attempted treason, and spying for a foreign country. Officials did not reveal which country Hanning allegedly worked for; the case that first surfaced the allegations involves an Israeli spy firm.

Hanning ran the Federal Intelligence Service from 1998 to 2005. After leaving his post, he allegedly recruited the agency’s chief of staff to hand him 2,000 classified documents beginning in 2010 in exchange for payment. Hanning maintained contact with two foreign countries’ intelligence services, including drafting an analysis for one of the countries, prosecutors say. He is Germany’s sole former intelligence chief known to face espionage charges; another high-ranking intelligence officer was accused of spying for Russia in 2023 in a trial that remains ongoing.

Prosecutors say they found the documents while investigating a German steakhouse heiress accused of faking the kidnapping of two of her children in a custody dispute. Christina Block allegedly employed an Israeli spy firm to help enact the plot; Hanning denies involvement.
