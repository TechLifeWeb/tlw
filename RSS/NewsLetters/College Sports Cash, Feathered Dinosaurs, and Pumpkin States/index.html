One Big Headline

Athletics Spending Spree

Northwestern University debuted its roughly $875M football stadium—the most expensive college stadium ever built—in a primetime matchup against Penn State last night (see renderings). The debut is part of a broader influx of money into college athletics.

The College Sports Commission this week announced that athletes received nearly $1.8B in revenue-sharing payments during the 2025-26 academic year. The system was established last year to settle lawsuits alleging that schools were denying college athletes a share of the commercial value they generated. A bill that passed the Senate this week would more than double the revenue-sharing cap, from roughly $20M per school to nearly $50M, with specific funds earmarked for player retention and women's and Olympic sports. It would also increase federal oversight over name, image, and likeness deals, which have generated over $582M in revenue for college athletes since June 2025.

The top 75 college athletic programs in the US were worth a combined $51.2B last year, up 13% from the previous year.

Quick Hits

G7 leaders agree to release up to 100 million barrels of diesel and crude oil.

The Group of Seven, a forum of leading industrialized democracies (see 101), has agreed to release stockpiled fuel to counter soaring energy prices. The release will occur over the next four months, starting with a substantial amount of diesel, which is crucial for long-haul trucking and farming. The average price of US diesel has surged 70%, to $6.37 per gallon, since the Iran war began in late February. (More)

US hiring slows and unemployment rises ahead of November midterms.

US employers added 29,000 jobs in September, below expectations of 84,000 and down sharply from the 133,000 added in August. The unemployment rate also ticked up 10 basis points to 4.2%, according to the final jobs report before the Nov. 3 midterm elections. A poll published Thursday found that 61% of adults say the national economy is worse off since President Trump took office in 2025. (More)

Congo's Ebola outbreak death toll surpasses 4,000 as unrest mounts.

The latest figures come as soldiers burned down an Ebola transit and treatment center in the Democratic Republic of the Congo this week while searching for weapons and people suspected of belonging to non-state armed groups (conflict 101). The fire forced 19,000 people to flee the camp. Meanwhile, healthcare workers gathered in a nearby city to protest unpaid wages. (More, w/charts)

Student protests in France close over 400 schools, lead to around 2,000 arrests.

The nationwide protests were spurred by overcrowded classrooms, poor facilities, and teacher shortages. Several have grown violent, with fires started in at least two schools and dozens reportedly injured. The demonstrations come as the French government this week proposed a below-inflation 1.7% increase to the education budget, amid wider austerity measures to rein in the nation's budget deficit. (More)

Tony Romo leaves CBS Sports as NFL analyst following OWI arrest.

The former Dallas Cowboys quarterback is parting ways with CBS Sports after nine years as a lead NFL game analyst. The 46-year-old was arrested during a July traffic stop in Milwaukee after performing poorly on a field sobriety test. He pleaded no contest last month and said he had developed an overreliance on alcohol to cope with pain from injuries sustained during his playing career. (More)

Feathered dinosaur fossil sheds light on the evolution of flight.

Researchers discovered a new feathered dinosaur species in northeastern China that could likely fly. However, certain bone features hint that it may have belonged to a distinct lineage from that of modern birds, indicating that flight may have evolved separately in different groups of dinosaurs.
