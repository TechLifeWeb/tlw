One Big Headline

The Amazon’s Living Map

Scientists and conservation groups launched an AI-powered monitoring network this week to track biodiversity and ecosystem health across the Amazon rainforest.

The project will monitor the roughly 2.7 million-square-mile Amazon River basin using satellites, acoustic sensors, and environmental DNA shed by species into their surroundings. Scientists will use AI to analyze these streams of information, potentially enabling them to detect patterns in near-real time. The international team of scientists leading the endeavor has raised $20M of the $100M needed to run the program for 10 years, including $14M from the Bezos Earth Fund. A smaller network of 65 stations in the Amazon has demonstrated the ability to track the Amazonian manatee and endangered river dolphins, which are threatened by mercury contamination largely from illegal gold mining.

Quick Hits

Supreme Court lifts restrictions on expansive voter-screening tool.

The justices ruled that the Trump administration can use a federal database to verify voter eligibility. A lower court had blocked the tool, saying it violated privacy rights and could result in voters being wrongfully removed from the rolls. The high court's ruling will likely have a limited impact on the November midterms, since federal law restricts most states from systematically removing voters from rolls within 90 days of an election. (More)

Separately, the Supreme Court rejected, for the third time, a Missouri congressional map backed by President Donald Trump, preventing its use in the midterms. (More)

New Jersey lieutenant governor resigns after ethics, harassment probe.

Lt. Gov. Dale Caldwell (D) resigned yesterday on calls from Gov. Mikie Sherill (D) after an ethics report found him in violation of workplace policies. Caldwell allegedly sought twice to promote his girlfriend to a state job and made sexual comments to a staffer. The timing of the resignation is significant, as Caldwell also served as New Jersey's secretary of state, overseeing elections, including November's midterms. (More)

Hurricanes Nolo and Polo strengthen in the Pacific, bringing heavy rain.

Residents of Hawaii's Big Island are bracing for up to 35 inches of rain and potentially life-threatening flooding after Nolo strengthened to a Category 2 storm off the south coast. Meanwhile, Category 5 Hurricane Polo could cause torrential flooding and landslides on Mexico's west coast. El Niño is warming surface waters in the tropical Pacific, increasing the likelihood of strong storms. (More)

Manchester City found guilty of breaching Premier League financial regulations.

The team was found guilty on all but one of 115 charges following a multiyear probe, including failing to provide accurate financial records and disclose coach and player payments. No sanctions have been decided, and the team is expected to appeal. Possible punishments range from a points deduction to expulsion from the league. (More)

Fraternity leaders arrested after pledges found injured in basement.

Two 20-year-olds were arrested after police received an anonymous tip about an alleged hazing incident at the University of Wisconsin-Madison's Alpha Epsilon Pi off-campus house. Thirty pledges were found partially clothed and covered in food, condiments, and other liquids. Some had visible injuries. (More)

Separately, police are investigating the deaths of two Ole Miss students found dead in separate incidents. A cause of death has not been determined, but kratom was found during both investigations. (More)
