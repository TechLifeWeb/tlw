Gloria Steinem Dies

Feminist activist, journalist, and author Gloria Steinem died Wednesday at her home in New York City at age 92, her foundation announced yesterday.

Steinem became one of the most recognizable leaders of the modern women’s movement, spending six decades advocating for gender equality, reproductive rights, and women’s political representation. Her national profile began with a 1963 exposé of working conditions at New York’s Playboy Club, where she spent 11 days undercover as a Bunny (see photos). She cofounded Ms. magazine in 1971—read her reflections on the first issue. She helped establish the National Women’s Political Caucus and Women’s Media Center and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2013. See how the movements she helped lead reshaped American life. Her final memoir, “An Unexpected Life,” is due out later this month.

Explore six decades of her personal archives and watch a 2008 interview with Steinem reflecting on her career.

Nvidia Stacks Up

Nvidia is purchasing Hugging Face—a repository of open-source AI models—for $12.9B, the company announced yesterday. The deal positions the world’s most valuable company to expand its control of the AI ecosystem beyond supplying semiconductor chips.

Hugging Face received a private-market valuation of $4.5B only three years ago. Since then, it has attracted more than 18 million users and 200,000 companies that use the platform to discover, share, and collaborate on more than 3 million open-source AI models (open- and closed-source models, explained). By purchasing the world’s biggest open-source AI model repository, Nvidia positions itself for a day when chip demand may slow. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, pending regulatory approval.

When Hugging Face was the subject of an OpenAI hack in July, the company closed the vulnerability using a Chinese open-source model. See five of the most fascinating takeaways from the hack.
