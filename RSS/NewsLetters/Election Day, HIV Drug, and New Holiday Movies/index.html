It's Election Day

Today is the final opportunity for Americans to cast their ballots in the midterm elections, with races for the US House, Senate, and state offices. Preliminary results will start trickling in as early as 6 pm ET, though official certification could take days or weeks. Find your local polling place here.

Early voting and campaign advertising spending are on track to break records this year, as voters cite the economy as their top concern. Final preelection polls largely show Democrats favored to retake the House and leading in at least five of seven states expected to decide the Senate: Michigan, Kansas, Texas, Maine, Ohio, Alaska, and Iowa (see dashboard). However, the polls may not accurately reflect outcomes, as Democrats have recently been more likely to respond than Republicans. Pollsters try to account for this by giving more statistical weight to Republican respondents, but 2016 and 2020 polls still underestimated Republican support. See how weighting works here.

HIV Drug Approval

The Food and Drug Administration approved a generic version of a twice-a-year injectable HIV prevention drug yesterday to be sold in 120 low- and middle-income countries. Generic lenacapavir will cost $40 per person per year in those countries; the brand-name version, by Gilead, which currently lists at over $28K before insurance coverage in the US.

Gilead's patent protection for lenacapavir in the US remains in effect, but FDA clearance of the generic will pave the way for faster regulatory approval in some other countries. The generic drug stems from voluntary licensing agreements Gilead signed with six manufacturers in 2024 to reach lower-income countries. The deals exclude some middle-income countries like Brazil, where HIV burden is nonetheless high.

Lenacapavir outperforms the daily oral protocol that has become the standard of care for reducing HIV transmission due to better adherence (watch via YouTube how the pill changed the course of HIV). An estimated 41 million people around the world live with HIV; roughly 570,000 died last year from HIV-related causes (see map).

Walking 'Round The World

Karl Bushby, a 57-year-old former British Army paratrooper who spent the last 28 years walking around the world, returned home yesterday. He covered approximately 31,000 miles, one of the longest geographically unbroken trek in history.

The journey began on Nov. 1, 1998, in Punta Arenas, Chile. Bushby, then 29, had plotted his path in the mid-'90s after a birthday card from his father sparked the idea of walking from South America’s southern tip to his hometown of Hull, England. He initially estimated he’d return in 12 years, but visa issues, the 2008 economic crash, and the pandemic hindered progress. An arrest in Russia and a near-fatal plunge into the Bering Strait’s freezing waters added hurdles. But Bushby never skipped sections or used transportation, opting to swim across the Caspian Sea and the English Channel. His expedition spanned four continents, 30 countries, and 10,229 days (scroll for map).
