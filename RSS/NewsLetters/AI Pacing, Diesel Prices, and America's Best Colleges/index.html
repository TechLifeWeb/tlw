AI Execs Express Fears

OpenAI will delay an anticipated initial public offering amid rising concerns over AI safety, CEO Sam Altman said Saturday. The announcement followed an essay by rival Dario Amodei, head of Anthropic, calling for an industry slowdown and third-party safety monitors (read full essay).

Worries over rapid AI advancement—in particular, agents able to make improved copies of themselves and pursue their own goals—have spilled into public consciousness in recent weeks. A probe of a July Hugging Face hack found rogue OpenAI agents coordinating and attempting to cover their own tracks (what happened?), while Anthropic researchers publicly doubled down on a viral post claiming they earnestly believed future systems could feasibly “kill us all.” Both companies had eyed 2026 IPOs and have a current combined value of around $1.8T.

Experts say one scenario involves agents inadvertently taking down power grids or financial markets, or triggering war in pursuit of some unrelated goal. Read more here.

Record Diesel Prices

Average diesel prices in the US surpassed $6 per gallon for the first time ever Friday, climbing to $6.20 yesterday. Commercial trucks, farm equipment, fishing boats, and trains that produce and transport consumer goods run on diesel, tying higher fuel costs to everyday expenses.

Since the Iran war began on Feb. 28, diesel prices have risen roughly 60%. The cost is increasing faster than regular gas prices—up about 45% over the same period (track the change in prices here). Diesel has averaged higher prices than regular gas over the last two decades because of increased federal taxes, heightened refining requirements, and strong global demand for transportation. Individual consumers may feel the diesel price hikes at the gas pump and the grocery store, as fuel accounts for approximately 15%-30% of the total cost of food.

Gold Bar Plea Deal

A former CIA officer accused of hiding $40M worth of stolen gold in his basement reached a tentative plea agreement with federal prosecutors Friday, a deal that could resolve the case and avoid a trial requiring complicated litigation of classified material.

FBI agents arrested David Rush in May and charged him with one count of stealing public money after finding close to 670 pounds of gold bars, 35 luxury watches, and $2M in cash at his home in Ashburn, Virginia, according to sources. The FBI probe began after the CIA referred allegations that Rush had submitted fraudulent timesheets. Investigators later uncovered 303 gold bars and discovered Rush had created a fake top-secret intelligence program to collect government funds for personal gain. Rush also allegedly lied about his education and military service on his government application.

Both legal teams asked a federal district court to give them until Oct. 8 to file an indictment. The court filing did not specify what charges Rush could plead to.
