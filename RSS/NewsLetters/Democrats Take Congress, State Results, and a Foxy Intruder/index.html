Democrats Sweep the Senate ...

US voters woke up this morning to a blue wave in the Senate. Republicans entered Election Day with a 53-47 majority; Democrats are projected to secure at least 54 seats as of this writing, with two races still uncalled.

In Texas, James Talarico (D) defeated Ken Paxton (R), helping end the party's 32-year losing streak in statewide elections. Talarico led 55%-43% as of this writing, with 93% of the vote counted. In South Carolina, Annie Andrews (D) ousted incumbent Sen. Darline Graham (R), winning by 4.4 points—the first time the state elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1998.

In Kansas, Adam Hamilton (D) unseated incumbent Sen. Roger Marshall (R) (51%-46%), becoming the first Democrat to win a Senate seat there since 1932. In Maine, Troy Jackson (D) defeated incumbent Sen. Susan Collins (R) (54%-46%), ending her 30-year term.

Roy Cooper (D) also flipped North Carolina, defeating Michael Whatley (R), while Josh Turek (D) flipped Iowa, defeating Ashley Hinson (R). In Ohio, former Sen. Sherrod Brown (D) defeated incumbent Sen. Jon Husted (R) to win back a seat on Capitol Hill.

In Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed (D) defeated Mike Rogers (R) by 6.6 points after one of the most contentious campaigns of the season. In Alaska, Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan led with 48.5% of the vote—but a final winner may not be known until the state's ranked-choice tabulation on Nov. 18.

In a final surprise in Mississippi, Scott Colom (D) leads incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith (R) by less than 100 votes. The state does not allow automatic recounts (though courts can order them).

... and the House

Democrats are projected to take control of the House, winning at least 228 seats as of this writing, with 68 races still uncalled. Projections estimate Democrats could secure more than 250 seats as votes are tallied.

The party needed to net at least four seats to overcome Republicans’ current 218-214 majority. Democrats flipped four key seats in swing state Pennsylvania alone, with Bob Harvie, Bob Brooks, Paige Cognetti, and Janelle Stelson ousting Republican incumbents.

In Ohio, Brian Poindexter (D) defeated incumbent Rep. Max Miller (R, OH-07), who is facing domestic abuse allegations. In a sign of the Democratic surge, Rep. Victoria Spartz (R, IN-5) lost to challenger JD Ford (D), despite winning in 2024 by 18 points.

Elsewhere, Democratic incumbents held several key districts redrawn by Republican-led states, including in Florida, North Carolina, Alabama and Texas. Roughly one-third of the nation’s congressional districts were redrawn this cycle (scroll for maps).

The president's party has lost seats in 18 of 20 midterm elections since 1946. Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D, NY-08) is poised to become the first Black speaker in US history when the new congressional session begins Jan. 3.

... and Key Down-Ballot Races

Thirty-six states held gubernatorial elections yesterday, with each party defending 18 seats and Republicans entering with a 26-24 edge nationwide. Among the notable results, Democrats flipped the Texas governorship for the first time in more than three decades after Republican Gov. Greg Abbott conceded his reelection bid to Democrat Gina Hinojosa.

In Ohio, Democrat Amy Acton defeated Republican Vivek Ramaswamy to succeed term-limited Gov. Mike DeWine (R), while Democrat Rob Sand beat Republican Zach Lahn in Iowa. Both victories marked the first time in 20 years that Democrats won gubernatorial elections in their respective states.

In Wisconsin, David Crowley (D) defeated Tom Tiffany (R) to succeed Gov. Tony Evers (D), becoming the state's first Black governor. In another first, Keisha Lance Bottoms (D) won Georgia's governorship, becoming the nation's first Black female governor and flipping a seat Republicans held for more than two decades.

Alaskans used ranked-choice voting to pick a successor to Gov. Mike Dunleavy (R), while also deciding whether to repeal the system; the 2024 repeal attempt failed by just 743 votes. Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins had 49% of the vote with 75% of ballots counted.

Separately, California considered a one-time wealth tax on billionaires, while voters weighed abortion-related ballot measures in Idaho, Nevada, Virginia, and Missouri. Several states also voted on voter ID requirements, and Idaho considered naming an official state gun. Dozens of local data center measures were also on ballots.
