One Big Headline

Turn off the Lights

Light exposure during nighttime sleep may lead to harmful changes in the heart's structure and function, according to a study published this week.

Researchers asked 11,071 participants without cardiovascular disease to wear light and motion sensors on their wrists for seven days to measure nighttime light exposure. Possible sources included streetlights and televisions. Participants underwent cardiac MRIs three years later, and those with greater exposure had thicker heart walls and weaker heartbeats. During eight- to 10-year follow-ups, those in the highest-exposure group had a 29% higher risk of heart failure, 24% higher risk of heart attack, 33% elevated risk of stroke, and 26% increased risk of cardiovascular death than those with the least exposure. See seven tips to limit nighttime light here.

Further research is needed to determine whether different types of light, such as LED versus fluorescent, have different effects. Future studies may also probe whether the changes stem from disrupted sleep or effects on the body's circadian rhythm.

Quick Hits

CIA releases intelligence sent to White House before 9/11.

The CIA yesterday released 69 never-before-seen briefs (download here) warning about Osama bin Laden and al-Qaida, including one warning then-President Bill Clinton about a potential plot to "fly an explosive-packed airplane into a US city.” Experts say the documents reaffirm that the US failed to respond to several warnings in the years leading up to 9/11. (More)

US inflation accelerates and diesel prices spike as war in Iran continues.

The inflation rose 3.4% in August compared with a year ago, unchanged from July and in line with analysts’ expectations. Interest-rate futures forecast about an 85% chance the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates next week, up from roughly 70% before the report. Meanwhile, diesel prices hit record highs above $6 a gallon, which is expected to hike the cost of shipping groceries and other goods. (More)

Iran-backed Houthis capture Red Sea island on vital oil shipping route.

The Houthi militia yesterday seized a Yemeni island at the choke point in the Red Sea, one day after taking control of a key Yemeni port. The moves tighten Iran and its allies' control of the Bab el-Mandab Strait, a vital oil shipping route. Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia shut down a major oil pipeline after a drone attack it claims originated in Iraq. The Iraqi government has ordered an investigation. (More, w/map)

Afghan woman deported in first-ever "alien terrorist" court case.

Nazira Haji Zada, a 47-year-old Afghan permanent resident living in Texas, opted yesterday to leave the US rather than continue litigating accusations that she supported an Islamic State-linked plot. She was the first person to appear before the Alien Terrorist Removal Court, which was established in 1996. The court allows the government to deport noncitizens whose case details cannot be publicly disclosed for national security reasons. (More)

CDC declares nation's largest-known cyclosporiasis outbreak over.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention declared that the outbreak ended yesterday, with a total of 12,883 lab-confirmed cases across 21 states since May. Officials identified at least one source—iceberg lettuce grown by Taylor Farms in Mexico—and are investigating at least five other potential sources. (More)

US Open wraps this weekend.

The world's new No. 1 women's tennis player, Elena Rybakina, faces the old No. 1 Aryna Sabalenka in the women's final today at 4 pm ET. This is Rybakina’s first US Open final, while Sabalenka is seeking her third straight title after winning in 2024 and 2025. Ben Shelton and Alexander Zverev will play tomorrow for the men's title. (More)

Justin Trudeau launches film and TV production company.

The former Canadian Prime Minister is launching Hope & Hard Work with his former chief of staff, Katie Telford. The company is developing adaptations of Perry Chafe's 2023 novel, "Closer by Sea"; Uzma Jalaluddin's 2025 novel "Detective Aunty"; and more.
