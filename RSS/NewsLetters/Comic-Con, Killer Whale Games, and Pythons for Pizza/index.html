Back to Hall H

Comic-Con International 2026 has kicked off in San Diego, bringing four days of studio announcements, celebrity panels, and fan events to one of the world's largest pop culture conventions. More than 135,000 attendees are expected through Sunday.

Founded in 1970 as a gathering of about 100 comic book fans in a hotel basement, Comic-Con now spans nearly 500,000 square feet of exhibit space and features more than 1,000 exhibitors and roughly 2,000 hours of programming, generating an estimated $160M for the local economy (watch how it evolved). Much of the action centers on Hall H, the convention's famed 6,500-seat venue for the biggest studio reveals, where fans often begin lining up two days in advance to secure a spot.

Marvel and DC Studios return to Hall H after both skipped last year's convention. Today's schedule includes a panel for "Spaceballs: The New One," a sequel that reunites original cast members with 100-year-old Mel Brooks nearly four decades after the original film. See Johnny Depp make an appearance as Ebenezer Scrooge and the complete schedule.

Sky-High Oil

Global oil prices topped $100 per barrel for the first time since May yesterday. The milestone came after Yemen’s Iranian-backed Houthi rebels said they attacked two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, where they recently announced a blockade.

The war now centers on the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, through which 12% to 15% of global maritime trade—and 7% of the world’s oil—passes (see map). Saudi Arabia can reroute some oil northward through the Suez Canal, but that route slows shipping to Asia by 10 to 15 days or more, and requires supertankers to reduce their oil capacity. With a new trade route threatened, President Donald Trump says he is weighing a “major military punishment” against Iran. Explore US gas prices here (w/map).

Separately, Congress was split yesterday on whether to end the Iran war: Four House Republicans joined Democrats to pass a nonbinding resolution while Senate Republicans blocked a binding measure.

Orcas Just Wanna Have Fun

Researchers this week published footage of orcas ramming into giant fish so forcefully that the prey explodes into tiny fragments. They believe the well-coordinated tactic may help younger orcas feed—or simply serve as entertainment.

Two separate encounters in the Gulf of California show one adult orca holding a dead sunfish in its mouth while another adult accelerates toward it. The fish is released just before the second orca strikes, sending pieces flying (watch here). Weighing up to 4,400 pounds, sunfish are among the world's largest fish and common prey for orcas, which can weigh up to 22,000 pounds. In both videos, younger orcas feed on the debris, suggesting the adults may be breaking the fish into bite-sized pieces for juveniles.

Scientists say the tactic could also just be a game. Orcas are known to do things that don't seem to serve practical purposes, like balancing dead salmon on their heads and swarming tourist boats.
