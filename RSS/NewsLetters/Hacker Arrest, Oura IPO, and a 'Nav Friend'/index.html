ShinyHunters Arrest

The FBI and Dutch National Police yesterday announced the arrest of an alleged member of the ShinyHunters cyber group, which claimed responsibility last week for hacking the FBI's jobs website.

The 24-year-old suspect was arrested on Sept. 15, a week before ShinyHunters said it stole two to three terabytes of data related to FBI employees and job applicants, including intelligence assignments and medical records. The hack was intended to pressure the bureau into retracting or revising this public advisory detailing alleged extortion tactics. It was posted days after the parent company of online learning platform Canvas paid ShinyHunters an undisclosed ransom following a hack that forced many colleges to postpone final exams. The FBI estimates ShinyHunters has breached more than 140 organizations and extorted $70M since last year.

Dutch authorities say information found on the suspect's laptop also revealed a plot to orchestrate two murders abroad, separate from his alleged ShinyHunters activity.

Oura IPO on Hold

Smart-ring maker Oura postponed its planned initial public offering yesterday, citing uncertain market conditions despite an expected valuation of roughly $15B. The move came after the company formally filed IPO paperwork earlier this month.

The delay comes despite strong growth at Oura, which reported $1.21B in revenue and $60.8M in profit for the nine months ending June 30 and expects fiscal 2026 revenue to grow 90%. The company this year reached 5.7 million paying members. Magnetic Field Far Away

Scientists have detected radio signals (free w/email) from a planet beyond our solar system for the first time ever. The finding, subject to peer review, does not imply an alien civilization is trying to contact Earth. Rather, it may indicate an unusually strong magnetic field on Beta Pictoris b, a planet more than 63 light-years away.

Several planets in our solar system naturally emit radio signals as charged particles move across their magnetic fields, creating auroras (see 101). The field protects a planet’s atmosphere from solar radiation, and also supports conditions for life. In Beta Pictoris b’s case (how are exoplanets named?), the field may be around 300 times as strong as Jupiter’s. It is not clear why the magnetic field would be so strong, but it could be due to the planet’s young age (25 million years old) or something else, like a moon orbiting it.
