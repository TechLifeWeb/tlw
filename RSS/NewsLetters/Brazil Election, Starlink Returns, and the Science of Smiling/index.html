Lula Wins in Brazil

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Brazil’s presidential runoff yesterday, edging out conservative populist Flávio Bolsonaro. Known by shorthand as “Lula,” the leftist incumbent overcame a two-point deficit to Bolsonaro after the first round of voting earlier this month.

The victory secures the 80-year-old Lula’s fourth presidential term. First elected in 2002, he left office at the end of 2010 due to constitutional limits on consecutive terms. He was jailed in 2018 on corruption charges linked to “Operation Car Wash”—a multibillion-dollar probe that began with a single small business. His convictions were nullified in 2021, allowing him to run against (and beat) Bolsonaro’s father, Jair, in 2022. Lula will have to contend with surging federal debt (about $1.8T) and frustration over public services and rising cost of living.

The race kicks off a string of major elections, including Benjamin Netanyahu seeking to maintain control in Israel (tomorrow, see polls) and US midterms next Tuesday.

Church Abuse Settlement

The Archdiocese of New York has agreed to pay more than $800M to settle decades of sexual abuse claims against clergy and staff, Catholic Church officials announced Friday. The agreement avoids a potential bankruptcy filing by the archdiocese and offers a minimum $250K lump-sum payout to roughly 1,300 plaintiffs (specific cases may seek higher compensation based on the extent of abuse).

While allegations persisted for years, a flood of legal filings followed the 2019 New York Child Victims Act. Along with a “lookback” window for expired claims, the law raised the age limit for victims to bring civil suits from 23 to 55—effectively reviving decades of cases. Similar settlements have been reached or finalized in Buffalo ($326M), Albany ($148M), Rochester ($246M), Syracuse ($176M), and Rockville Centre ($323M).

Since a pivotal 2002 Boston Globe investigation that brought church abuse into the spotlight (see overview), major settlements have tallied more than $5B and surfaced at least 16,000 abuse allegations.

Starlink Slowdown Standoff

Elon Musk accused Ukraine on Saturday of violating Starlink rules by using the technology for offensive drones. The remarks are Musk’s first acknowledgment of an active role in restricting Kyiv’s satellite communications system this month.

Starlink is a civilian communications service, while its counterpart, Starshield, is a Pentagon-funded program. Ukraine relies on both systems in its war with Russia but denies the ability to use Starlink for offensive capabilities. When Ukraine first reported communications disruptions, Musk suggested the US government directed the slowdown. A Reuters report indicated the decision came from the White House amid tensions over Ukraine’s targeting of Russian oil infrastructure (see previous write-up). US officials denied the allegations. President Donald Trump reportedly called Musk last week requesting SpaceX restore Ukraine’s full access to Starlink and Starshield; Ukraine has confirmed both systems are functioning normally.

Trilateral negotiations between the US, Russia, and Ukraine are due to resume next month. See war updates here.
