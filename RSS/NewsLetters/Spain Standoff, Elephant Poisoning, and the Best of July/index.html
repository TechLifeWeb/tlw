Moroccan Migrant Surge

Spain deployed its army to an autonomous Spanish city on the North African coast yesterday after an estimated 2,000 to 3,000 migrants swam, walked, and climbed across its border with Morocco (watch video). At least nine people have died.

Local officials say the influx is overwhelming migrant reception centers. The cause of the surge is unclear, but Ceuta and the smaller Spanish territory of Melilla are the European Union and Africa's only land border. A 2024 survey found that roughly 35% of Moroccans were considering leaving the country, with 45% citing economic reasons. Morocco's unemployment rate is around 10%, compared to a 4.8% global average. The scenes also echo a May 2021 border crisis, when more than 8,000 migrants entered Ceuta over two days after Morocco relaxed border controls in a move widely seen as a political protest.

While Spain has controlled Ceuta since 1580, Morocco also claims sovereignty over the roughly 7-square-mile city, based on geographical proximity and historical ties.

Shooter's Father Sentenced

The father of a Georgia teenager who carried out the deadly 2024 Apalachee High School shooting was sentenced to 15 years in prison yesterday. Colin Gray was convicted of second-degree murder and involuntary manslaughter for gifting his son, Colt, the assault rifle used in the attack. Colt, now 16, was sentenced to life in prison earlier this week.

Prosecutors said the elder Gray disregarded warning signs, including his son’s interest in mass shootings and a shrine his son erected in his bedroom to the Parkland shooter. The gift also came despite a home visit by law enforcement months earlier when authorities received a tip Colt had threatened to kill students at an elementary school (see police camera footage).

The case is one of a handful in which parents of teenage shooters were held criminally responsible for their kids’ actions. That includes Michigan’s James and Jennifer Crumbley, who were sentenced to at least 10 years in prison for failing to secure a newly purchased gun and ignoring warning signs.

Deadly Elephant Poisoning

Fifteen elephants in southern Kenya have died over the past month after eating cyanide-laced tomatoes left near farms bordering Amboseli National Park, home to roughly 1,900 elephants, according to local authorities.

Laboratory tests detected the chemical, used in agriculture for pest control and sometimes by poachers, in the animals' bodies. Most became paralyzed before collapsing within two days of symptom onset. Authorities have not identified suspects, but officials believe local farmers may have poisoned the tomatoes to deter elephants from raiding crops. Elephants can consume up to 600 pounds of food each day and often wander onto farmland during dry seasons when natural forage is scarce (and perhaps to self-medicate). Kenya has documented previous elephant poisonings tied to poaching and human-wildlife conflict, but officials say this is the region's deadliest such incident in decades. Officials warned livestock grazing in the contaminated area could also be at risk, though they said the toxins pose no immediate threat to people.
