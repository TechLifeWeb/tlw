USPS Whistleblower Claims

A whistleblower warned Congress yesterday that a rushed US Postal Service system stemming from President Donald Trump’s effort to impose new federal requirements on mail voting could prevent legitimate ballots from reaching voters ahead of November’s midterm elections.

The system, temporarily blocked by a federal judge, would require election officials to upload voter information and ballot bar codes to a federal portal before ballots are mailed, giving USPS a new role in verifying who may receive them—a process traditionally handled by state and local officials (see what’s different). The whistleblower alleges USPS built the system in roughly three months, despite such projects typically taking roughly a year. A single bar code failing a sample check could cause an entire batch to be rejected and returned to election officials. Roughly 30% of Americans voted by mail in 2024; see how mail-ballot deadlines vary by state.

A Glimmer of Dark?

Hundreds of physicists are poring over a finding that may signal the first-ever detection of dark matter—or is otherwise difficult to explain using any known particle. They published their findings yesterday in the hope of gathering input from scientists across the world.

Dark matter accounts for about 85% of the matter in the universe. We cannot see or touch it, but its existence is considered necessary to explain how galaxies rotate, among other phenomena (watch our favorite explainer). To detect dark matter, scientists filled a tank with liquid xenon and placed it a mile underground. The goal was to minimize, isolate, and identify particles passing through. Visible particles would predictably release light and electrons; however, there is one case—recorded in June 2023—where a particle released energy on a scale suggesting its mass is more than 200 times that of a proton.

Researchers say there is a 0.5% chance the recording could be explained by other known physical properties. Go deeper with one potential dark matter candidate: supersymmetric particles.

Russian Drones in Germany

The German government accused Russia yesterday of directing three drones to Leipzig/Halle Airport in the eastern town of Schkeuditz last month. Germany's second-largest cargo airport serves as a key logistics hub for shipments bound for Ukraine.

On Aug. 4, an airport employee found a drone carrying military-grade explosives near a Ukrainian cargo plane parked in a secure area. A second drone hit a different cargo plane approaching the airport. A third landed in a nearby field. None of the drones detonated. Germany’s investigation revealed elements of the attack were consistent with hybrid operations Russia has employed against Ukraine. In response, the government announced yesterday that it is tightening restrictions on Russians entering the country, terminating the lease of a Russian cultural center in Berlin, and closing its last Russian consulate. The Kremlin denies responsibility.
