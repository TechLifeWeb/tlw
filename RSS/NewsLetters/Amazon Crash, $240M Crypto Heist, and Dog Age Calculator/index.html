Amazon Plane Crash

Federal investigators yesterday said they have begun collecting evidence from an Amazon cargo plane that overshot the runway at Miami International Airport by roughly 1,300 feet Sunday. The incident killed at least five people, injured at least five others, and shut down all runways for several hours.

A video (warning—sensitive) appears to show the Boeing 767 barreling across a small public road at nearly 130 miles per hour, striking several vehicles. The Federal Aviation Administration says the runways at the Miami airport are not equipped with a crushable arresting system designed to stop planes that overshoot because the airport's runways have a 1,000-foot safety buffer. The system, installed at 70 airports, has helped stop 26 aircraft carrying 497 people.

The flight was operated by 21 Air, one of nine vendors Amazon uses to ship customer packages on more than 250 daily flights. Go behind the scenes of Amazon's air cargo operations here (w/video).

‘Ferris Bueller Gone Bad’

The alleged ringleader of one of the largest cryptocurrency heists in the US is due in court today for his plea agreement hearing. Malone Lam, a 22-year-old Singaporean citizen in California, stands accused of siphoning more than $240M worth of bitcoin with the help of 17 others.

Lam and his network met in online gaming forums and allegedly began scamming people out of cryptocurrency in 2023. In one instance, they targeted a cryptocurrency investor through two phone calls, each warning about security threats to his cryptocurrency wallet, in order to access his security codes. Defendants allegedly spent the ill-gotten gains on luxury cars and watches, private jet rides, rented mansions, and nightclub benders. Lam allegedly spent more than $569K in one night at a Los Angeles nightclub. One judge likened the case to “Ferris Bueller gone bad.”

Cryptocurrency fraud reports jumped up last year, with Americans reporting losses of more than $11B. See tips to avoid phone scams.

Russia-North Korea Road Link

Russia and North Korea opened the first road bridge across their shared border yesterday. The new link over the Tumen River comes as the countries have expanded a strategic partnership amid Russia’s invasion of Ukraine (scroll for map).

The two-lane bridge is 0.6 miles long and will allow up to 300 vehicles to cross per day, beginning with cargo trucks. The countries agreed to the construction during Russian President Vladimir Putin’s trip to North Korea in 2024. Putin and North Korean leader Kim Jong Un also signed a defense pact that year requiring each country to provide military assistance if the other is attacked. North Korea has supplied Russia with ammunition, missiles, and thousands of troops for the war in Ukraine.

The bridge adds a third form of transport between the countries, joining existing air and rail routes. Direct flights offer rare travel opportunities to Russian tourists, including access to a North Korean beach resort (see inside, via YouTube).
