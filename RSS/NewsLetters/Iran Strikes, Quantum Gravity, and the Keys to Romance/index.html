Iran Strikes Escalate

Iran's foreign ministry accused the US of a war crime yesterday after shrapnel from a US missile hit a home hosting a wedding in Kuhestak, east of the Strait of Hormuz. The attack reportedly killed four people and wounded over 60 (see aftermath via YouTube, warning—sensitive).

US Central Command said it struck Iranian military targets along the country’s southern coast Tuesday to counter Tehran’s attacks on commercial ships and US forces. The attacks resulted in 14 other fatalities. Iran responded by targeting US military bases in Jordan, Iraq, and Kuwait. Most of the missiles and drones were intercepted, and no casualties were reported as of this writing. The exchange ended a monthslong pause in military action, during which the US sought to isolate Iran through economic sanctions.

Meanwhile, the USS Abraham Lincoln docked in Thailand yesterday for a port call after 286 days at sea (see previous write-up). The roughly 5,000-person crew will remain there until Sunday, then return to San Diego.

Meta Glasses Crackdown

Meta announced new measures yesterday to block people using its AI-powered smart glasses from filming others without their knowledge. The effort comes as demand for Meta Glasses has skyrocketed, alongside a wave of videos showing men harassing women in public.

The company’s partnerships with Ray-Ban and Oakley have succeeded where other smart glasses endeavors have failed: Last year, Meta sold more than 7 million pairs—more than triple the 2 million sold in 2023 and 2024. The market has skewed young and male (see demographics), with Meta accounting for roughly 82% of global sales. However, some have criticized how easy it is for users to film people without their consent, including by covering up a light indicating recording. Now, Meta says cameras will be disabled if the glasses detect the light has been covered.

Apple and Snap are developing competing products amid predictions the sector will grow more than 60% by 2029. Google is also reentering the market, years after the failure of Google Glass (what went wrong?).

Einstein Theory Prevails

A falling quantum object is subject to the effects of gravity, a new study found yesterday. The finding for the first time confirms a decades-old prediction by Albert Einstein, aligning with his broader theory of gravity.

Physicists sought to probe whether Einstein’s equivalence principle—whereby objects undergo weightlessness in free fall—applies on a quantum level (see 101). To test the question, they used an apparatus that split a quantum wave associated with an atom into two paths, holding one still while projecting the other into free fall. After the experiment, interference between the waves allowed researchers to record a tiny difference in quantum phase aligning with calculations anticipated by Einstein’s theory of gravity. The finding marks the first direct measurement of its kind and suggests that the equivalence principle continues to apply at the quantum level.

While novel, the study does not reconcile the fields of quantum and classical physics. Learn about some of the differences here.
