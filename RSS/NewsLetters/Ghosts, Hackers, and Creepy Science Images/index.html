Humans have long wondered what comes after death, and across history, one answer has endured: ghosts, the spirits of the dead lingering among the living (see the video overview). While there is no scientific evidence that supernatural beings are real, proving or disproving their existence is generally considered impossible.

> Discover what percentage of Americans believe in spirits and the afterlife. (More)
> See if your city hosts one of the most haunted places in the United States. (More)

Mentions of spirits date back to ancient times, and ghost tales have been explored across cultures, continents, and periods, with rituals that still exist to connect with them and ward them off. The places most commonly reported as haunted are sites like burial grounds, asylums, hotels, castles, and buildings where tragedy struck.

> Ouija boards used to be a wholesome group activity, until the film "The Exorcist" changed public perception. (More)

> Experts believe that ghost stories help us process death, loss, and grief. (More, w/video)

The idea of the soul, immortal and separate from the body, is held across different religions. Some psychologists believe that religion evolved to soothe common anxieties related to death and the afterlife. Spiritualism, the belief that the dead can talk to the living, captured the US and Britain in the mid-1800s, drawing séance guests from Queen Victoria to Charles Darwin.
