Fed Hikes Rates

The Federal Reserve raised interest rates for the first time since 2023 yesterday, announcing a quarter-percentage-point increase in benchmark rates and bringing the range to between 3.75% and 4%. Stocks closed down on the news (S&P 500 -0.5%, Dow -1.2%, Nasdaq -0.0%). See Federal Reserve 101 here.

The rise marks Chair Kevin Warsh’s first effort to combat inflation, which has remained above the bank’s 2% target for over five years. The vote, which was unanimous, came despite calls by President Donald Trump to lower rates or see him end trade with dozens of countries—any with which the US has a deficit, including China, Mexico, Vietnam, and the EU (see map). Instead, the bank signaled the possibility of at least one more rate increase this year.

Long-term Treasury yields had already priced in the expected hike and did not move significantly on the news. See how credit card debt, mortgages, and other household expenses are impacted by yesterday’s decision.

Human-Boosted Mice Brains

Scientists have transplanted lab-grown human brain cells into bioengineered mice, a study revealed yesterday. The advancement may offer a new way to study certain brain disorders and could pave the way for similar tests in larger animals.

Stanford researchers genetically engineered mice to remove most of their cerebral cortex, the gray matter responsible for reasoning and logic (see a 3D human brain). They then reprogrammed human skin cells into cortical brain tissue to insert into 29 newborn mice. The human cells grew to fill over 90% of the mice’s cortical brain tissue by volume and formed connections with the mice’s nervous systems. Those mice behaved mostly like their nonengineered counterparts except when deprived of oxygen, affecting their ability to walk. Researchers hope the altered mice can be used to test treatments for neurological conditions that are linked to oxygen deprivation in a developing brain, like cerebral palsy.

To maintain ethical boundaries, scientists concluded the study once the human cells were around six months old—before markers of consciousness emerged.

Canada Eyes Europe

The European Union yesterday invited Canada to become the bloc's first associate member, as both are experiencing tensions with the US. The status has never before existed; terms of the arrangement will be negotiated next month.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney says associate membership could expand trade between Canada and the EU. About 70% of Canadian exports go to the US (visualize here), which imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20B in goods after talks collapsed last month. The arrangement could also let Canadians live and work in EU countries without visas, while deepening partnerships on Arctic security, critical minerals, AI, defense, and more. Canada recently became the first non-EU country to join a roughly $173B defense program, created partly to reduce reliance on American weapons after the Trump administration reduced support for NATO.

The proposal comes as new polling shows that four in five Canadians want Canada to be closer to the EU.
