New Nvidia CEO

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned yesterday amid reports of extramarital affairs and an investigation into whether he was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the company searches for a successor.

Huang faced mounting pressure after admitting to infidelity amid reports linking him to Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom US officials suspect of spying for China. On Wednesday, The New York Times reported a second affair with a senior executive who reported directly to Huang, while Reuters reported allegations of a third affair yesterday, after his resignation. Independent directors and outside counsel are examining potential espionage risks, while the FBI is conducting a separate inquiry. Nvidia has lost more than $550B in market value since its board announced an investigation Sunday. (Explore a timeline of the scandal.)

Huang cofounded Nvidia in 1993, transforming it into an artificial intelligence powerhouse. Hear how he went from a Denny's dishwasher to one of tech's most influential leaders—and see how his departure could shake the entire AI industry.

Brooklyn Building Collapse

Rescue crews finished searching the rubble of a collapsed Brooklyn apartment building yesterday, as the death toll rose to eight people. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani (D) said two children had been killed and at least 18 people suffered injuries.

The three-story building crumbled into a neighboring construction site at around 4 pm local time Wednesday (see photos). Firefighters arrived on scene within one minute of the 911 call, and over 250 emergency personnel worked overnight to free trapped survivors. Watch video (warning—sensitive). Heavy rain hindered rescue efforts yesterday, muddying the collapse site and grounding two police drones. Records show the adjacent construction area had been cited for violations in March, including unauthorized demolition work and inadequate protection to shield other buildings. The city’s Department of Buildings is investigating what caused the collapse, and Brooklyn’s district attorney’s office is investigating whether criminal conduct was a factor.

Depp’s Box Office Test

Johnny Depp returns to the big screen as Ebenezer Scrooge in Paramount Pictures' adaptation of Charles Dickens' "A Christmas Carol," out today (see trailer). It marks Depp's first major Hollywood role since his legal battle against ex-wife Amber Heard, who accused him of domestic abuse.

"Ebenezer," which had a $38M production budget, is expected to bring in roughly $40M worldwide during its opening weekend. The film's performance is widely seen as a test of Depp's ability to draw audiences following a 2020 libel trial in the UK and 2022 defamation trial in the US related to Heard's abuse allegations (see timeline). Depp, who denies the allegations, is reportedly taking a pay cut for "Ebenezer," earning $5M compared with $16M in the 2018 "Fantastic Beasts." Disney is also reportedly evaluating a revival of the "Pirates of the Caribbean" franchise with Depp.

Dickens' 1843 novella has inspired over 100 adaptations, which literary experts credit to its simple structure and timeless message.
