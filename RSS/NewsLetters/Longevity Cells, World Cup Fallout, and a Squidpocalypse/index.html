One Big Headline

Supercentenarian Secret

People age 100 and older appear to have elevated levels of a rare immune cell that kills cancerous cells, according to a study published this week. The finding suggests that late-age immune adaptations may play a role in longevity.

Researchers in Japan tested blood samples from 28 adults divided into three age groups: 70 to 99, 100 to 109, and 110 and older. They found that the older participants, on average, exhibited a higher ratio of CD4 CTLs, a rare immune cell also found in people fighting cancer (immune system 101, w/video). However, these older participants showed no signs of cancer. The researchers believe elevated levels of CD4 CTLs may signal a new stage of immune development that emerges around age 100 and helps the body eliminate abnormal cells, which become increasingly common with age.

Yesterday was the 117th birthday of the world's oldest living person, an Englishwoman named Ethel Caterham. The oldest living person on record died in 1997 at the age of 122 years and 164 days.

Quick Hits

Hong Kong's Tiananmen activists convicted in national security trial.

Hong Kong's High Court yesterday convicted two pro-democracy activists of inciting subversion for organizing annual vigils remembering Beijing's 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown. Such vigils were effectively outlawed in 2020 with the enactment of a China-imposed National Security Law, which criminalizes a wide range of dissenting acts. The activists face a maximum of 10 years in prison. (More)

IndyCar race hits the streets around DC's National Mall this weekend.

The first-ever motor race in the nation's capital is part of celebrations for the US' 250th birthday. Top drivers, including 2026 Indianapolis 500 winner Felix Rosenqvist, will race 147 laps around a 1.66-mile course at speeds up to 180 mph (take a ride along the track here). Preservationists have raised concerns that vibrations from the race could damage paintings in museums and historic buildings. (More)

FIFA suspends four players, one official for World Cup final brawl.

Argentine midfielder Leandro Paredes was fined $90K and suspended for 10 matches, effectively sidelining him from Argentina's national team for a year. It ranks alongside a 2014 sanction against a Uruguayan player as one of the most severe in World Cup history. Two of Paredes' teammates, one Argentine coach, and one Spanish player received lesser sanctions. Watch a clip of the altercation here. (More)

Supreme Court allows White House ballroom construction to continue.

Chief Justice John Roberts yesterday issued a temporary stay allowing President Donald Trump's roughly $400M White House ballroom renovation project to proceed as the high court weighs his appeal. Preservationists have sued, arguing that the project requires congressional approval. Meanwhile, Trump argues the renovation is necessary for security, citing recent shootings and threats in his vicinity. (More)

Former Abercrombie & Fitch CEO mentally fit to stand sex trafficking trial.

A New York judge yesterday ruled that Michael Jeffries is competent to stand trial after being hospitalized for months with dementia and Alzheimer's symptoms. The 82-year-old is slated to go on trial alongside two codefendants in late October on allegations that they promised modeling jobs to lure men into drug-fueled sex parties. Jeffries led Abercrombie from 1992 to 2014. (More)

Court slashes $50M judgment against Infowars' Alex Jones.

A Texas appeals court yesterday reduced punitive damages against Jones over his claims that the 2012 Sandy Hook school shooting was a hoax to $1.5M. The court ruled that the two parents who sued failed to show sufficient evidence that the harassment stemming from his claims justified exceeding the state's $750K damages cap per plaintiff. The ruling does not affect a $1.4B judgment against Jones in Connecticut. (More)

Rare tornadoes hit New York City area amid East Coast storms.

Tornadoes struck southern Queens, Long Island, and Delaware on Thursday night, damaging cabanas and buildings at a Long Island beach club. Two to three inches of rain were also reported across the area, with several reports of people trapped in their cars by flooding. No serious injuries have been reported as of this writing. Tornadoes are rare in the area because the Atlantic Ocean typically limits atmospheric instability. (More, w/video)

Depression stops the formation of new brain cells, according to new study.

New neurons appear to stop forming in the hippocampus of adults with major depressive disorder. Because the hippocampus plays an important role in memory and emotional response, researchers believe this lack of new neurons may make it harder to adapt to stress. The finding challenges a long-held belief that depression stems solely from an imbalance in neurotransmitters and points to a potential new therapeutic target. (More)
