Tennessee Execution Failure

A Tennessee woman convicted of a 1995 murder is hospitalized in critical condition after apparently surviving two doses of lethal injection Wednesday night. Gov. Bill Lee (R) paused executions as the state reviews its protocols.

Christa Pike, 50, would have been the first woman executed in Tennessee in over 200 years. She spent 30 years on death row after being convicted of murdering a classmate at age 18. Her lawyers spent decades appealing her sentence, citing a history of sexual trauma, bipolar disorder, and concerns about injection delivery into her small veins. Tennessee delayed another execution in May after medical staff could not find a vein. Pike is believed to be the first person to survive two doses of the drug pentobarbital (why is it controversial?). The state does not have a protocol for administering a third dose.

Lethal injections have become the most common execution method in the US (see map), but also have the highest failure rate, at 7.2%.

School Voucher Rules

The Treasury Department yesterday unveiled proposed rules for the nation’s first federal tax-credit scholarship program, set to launch Jan. 1 and help cover privateand public-school expenses.

Created under last year’s tax and spending law, the program offers taxpayers a nonrefundable, dollar-for-dollar federal tax credit of up to $1,700 annually—or $3,400 for married couples filing jointly—for donations to approved scholarship-granting organizations, which distribute funds to eligible families. Scholarships can cover private school tuition, tutoring, disability services, books, computers, and other education expenses. Families earning up to three times their area’s median income qualify, covering roughly 96% of students in participating states. The rules let states select which qualifying SGOs participate, but limit additional restrictions on how they distribute funds and require annual independent audits. At least 31 states have said they will participate (see where your state stands).

By 2030, officials estimate the program could generate nearly $26B annually, funding about 2.2 million scholarships. See how school vouchers work and the debate surrounding them.

How Old Do I Sound?

Scientists have developed a new AI-powered tool that uses speech to estimate age and indicate signs of cognitive issues like dementia. The so-called “speech clock” could make aging assessments more accessible in rural communities because it does not require advanced medical technologies or blood samples.

Researchers recorded 2,928 Spanish-speaking participants, using audio samples from healthy adults to participants with dementia, including Alzheimer’s disease. Scientists then used machine-learning algorithms to extract over 700 speech characteristics that can evolve with age, including pitch, vocabulary range, and talking speed. The data informed the speech clock’s assessments of participants’ ages and resulted in the tool identifying when a voice sounded older than the speaker. Larger gaps between the ages correlated with higher levels of a blood-based biomarker that can detect Alzheimer's with roughly 90% accuracy.
