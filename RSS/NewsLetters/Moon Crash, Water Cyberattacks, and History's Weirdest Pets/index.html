The Moon's New Scar

A discarded SpaceX rocket segment was on track to slam into the moon this morning at around 2:35 am ET, likely creating a crater about 16 feet deep and 65 to 100 feet wide. The unintentional impact offers scientists a rare chance to study human-made lunar collisions, which could become more common with the rise of space debris.

The 45-foot-long, nearly 10,000-pound second stage helped launch two lunar landers in January 2025 (scroll for rocket diagram). Atmospheric drag was supposed to pull the stage back into Earth’s atmosphere afterward, but SpaceX says conditions including solar activity redirected it toward the moon. This morning's collision will likely have occurred near the Einstein Crater at a speed of roughly 5,400 miles per hour—more than twice that of a rifle bullet—releasing as much force as about 15,000 sticks of dynamite.

The SpaceX collision is the second known accidental human-made impact on the moon, following a 2022 crash by a Chinese rocket segment that created a pair of craters (w/photos).

US Water Targeted

At least 12 states have reported cyberattacks targeting drinking water and wastewater utilities, with officials investigating whether Iranian-linked hackers were behind some of the incidents. Authorities say drinking water was not contaminated, though some Minnesota residents experienced low-pressure water flow or received boil water notices.

The attacks targeted internet-connected control systems used to manage pumps, wells, valves, and water pressure rather than the water itself. More than 30 systems in Minnesota and nine in Michigan have reported compromises, while utilities in at least 10 other states have also been affected. Officials say hackers changed passwords, altered network settings, and temporarily locked operators out of control systems, but built-in safeguards and manual operations prevented contamination. Iranian-linked groups have previously targeted US water facilities and other US organizations.

There are over 152,000 public water systems in the US. See how hackers can turn critical infrastructure into a weapon—and how a raindrop can travel miles before it reaches your tap.

Arson Arrest

Washington authorities have arrested a man accused of sparking the Old Trails fire, one of three wildfires in Spokane that are 0% contained as of this writing. The fires have collectively burned more than 16 square miles, destroying more than 700 houses and structures and forcing more than 65,000 people to evacuate.

The suspect is 37-year-old Aaron Farinacci, previously convicted for killing his father in 2010. He was identified by a witness who says they saw him bent by the side of the road on Saturday near where the fire started. Farinacci is accused of using a match or lighter to spark the fire and was charged with first-degree arson. Authorities are still investigating the cause of the other two fires.
