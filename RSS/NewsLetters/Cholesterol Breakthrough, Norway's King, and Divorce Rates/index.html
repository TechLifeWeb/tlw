CRISPR Takes on Cholesterol

An experimental gene-editing treatment lowered "bad" cholesterol and triglycerides in patients for at least a year after a single dose, according to new results released yesterday from an early-stage trial. Among those receiving the highest dose, bad cholesterol fell by an average of 53% and triglycerides by 48%.

The treatment uses CRISPR—a technology that allows scientists to make targeted changes to DNA—to switch off a gene in liver cells that helps regulate blood fats. Initial results released last November showed the approach worked for at least two months; the latest follow-up suggests the effect may be longer lasting. The trial involved 15 patients with severe cholesterol or triglyceride disorders. Researchers reported no serious treatment-related side effects, though larger trials are needed to assess long-term safety and effectiveness. Watch how CRISPR works.

Cholesterol is a waxy substance that comes in two forms, low- and high-density lipoproteins (LDL and HDL), while triglycerides are energy stored as fat in the bloodstream. Too much LDL, or "bad" cholesterol, can contribute to plaque buildup in blood vessels, increasing the risk of heart attack and stroke.

Nepal flood death toll nears 600 as rescue efforts continue.

More than 3,700 people have been rescued as crews search for survivors following Wednesday's massive flood along the Nepal-China border. At least 579 people have been killed in Nepal and five in Tibet, while roughly 2,500 remain missing; authorities are monitoring newly formed lakes upstream for risks of further flooding. Norway's King Harald V dies at 89 after 35 years on the throne.

Harald died yesterday after being hospitalized with a rare blood disorder and later developing a bacterial bloodstream infection. The monarch, who took the throne in 1991, is succeeded by his 53-year-old son, Haakon; Haakon's 22-year-old daughter, Crown Princess Ingrid Alexandra, is now heir to the throne. Dolly Parton's music streams surge more than 2,000% after her death.

US audio and video streams of Parton's catalog jumped from 1.2 million Monday to 30.5 million Tuesday, the day the country music icon died at age 80. "Jolene" led the surge, climbing from roughly 196,000 streams to 3.3 million, while "9 to 5" jumped to 3 million. "Islands in the Stream" and "I Will Always Love You" each topped 2 million.
