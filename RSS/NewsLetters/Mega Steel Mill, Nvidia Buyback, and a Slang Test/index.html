Steeling Up in Iowa

President Donald Trump unveiled plans yesterday for a $15B steel mill project in Iowa, which he says will be the largest such plant in US history. The announcement comes as Trump seeks to drum up support for his economic agenda ahead of the November midterms.

The plant will be built by Mesabi Metallics, a Minnesota-based company owned by Indian conglomerate Essar Group. It aims to start production in 2030, ramping up to 10 million tons of steel per year and supporting 1,750 permanent new jobs (visualize steel production by country). The company will transport iron ore from its new $2.5B Minnesota mine, allowing for fully US-made steel. The project supports Trump’s effort to reduce the US trade deficits and is expected to lower costs to US consumers from Trump’s associated steel tariff regime.

The announcement comes amid a newly competitive race in Iowa, where Democrats have not won the Senate in 18 years.

Nvidia's Big Buyback

Nvidia announced plans yesterday to spend another $150B buying back its own shares, bringing its total buyback authorization to $235B—the largest in history. The company's shares closed up 1.7% on the news.

The buyback would reduce the number of Nvidia shares on the market, potentially boosting the company's stock value. Experts say Nvidia is signaling that it believes its shares are undervalued, given its status as the world's most valuable publicly traded company, with a market capitalization of over $5.4T. The buyback increase comes after the tech giant reported its second-quarter revenue hit $96.2B, a 106% gain from the same period last year.

The company also launched a security platform yesterday that can intervene if AI agents attempt unauthorized activity, such as hacking. Nvidia said over 100 companies, including Microsoft and Citi, are already using the software. Meanwhile, regulators are racing to assign liability for increasingly common AI-driven cyberattacks.

Air Base Threat Thwarted

British police released five men on bail yesterday after arresting them on suspicion of plotting a terrorist attack near a US-operated air force base. Officials are investigating possible links to Iran, Russia, or nonstate militant groups.

The men, all British nationals in their mid-20s, were arrested early Sunday after police responded to a call from a local farmer describing oddly parked vans and men wearing face coverings (read her account). Eighty-five homes were evacuated while bomb-detection robots examined the vans; their findings are unclear as of this writing. The UK began allowing the US to use a nearby air force base to house bombers flying missions against Iran in March, following Iranian strikes on British allies and shipping interests. Iran's Revolutionary Guard has said the base is a legitimate target but denied responsibility for the incident.

More than 75% of British citizens are concerned about the Iran war's impact on British national security, according to a poll conducted days after the UK approved US use of its base.
